Privacy

← App

The Terrariums app is designed to work without an account and without any server of ours. This page describes precisely where the data goes, so that you can check what follows in the source code, published on GitHub. The French version (Confidentialité) is the authoritative text.

What the app records, and where

  • On your device only (browser local storage): the address of your MQTT broker, the username and password you typed there, the list of your bases with their pairing code, your language choice, and the temperatures received over time to draw the charts over several days. None of this leaves your device, except towards the broker you chose. Uninstalling the app or clearing the site data deletes it. If you set a lock code, the broker password and the pairing codes are encrypted with that code (AES-256); the code itself is stored nowhere.
  • At your MQTT broker provider (for example HiveMQ Cloud, if that is the one you chose): the messages exchanged between your bases and the app go through that service, under its own terms and its own privacy policy. They contain the temperatures, the setpoints, the commands and each base's identifier. The pairing code is sent in every command, over an encrypted link (TLS) — which is why the app refuses unencrypted addresses.
  • At the site's host (Netlify): the usual technical logs of a web server (IP address, requested page, date), kept by the host for the security of the service. The site sets no cookie and embeds no audience measurement tool.

What the app does not do

  • No account creation, no e-mail address requested.
  • No cookie, no tracker, no advertising, no third-party script.
  • No data is sent to the app's publisher. The diagnostics report is only produced if you click "Copy the report", and you decide whom to send it to; it contains no password and no pairing code.

If you use a Terrariums server (optional)

The repository provides a self-hosted server (serveur/) that replaces the bare broker. It is not operated by the app's publisher: whoever installs it becomes responsible for it. Such a server records, on its own machine: the accounts (e-mail address and password hash), the list of bases attached to each account, the bases' temperature history (adjustable retention), the push notification subscriptions (technical addresses provided by the browser, never the content of the phone's other notifications) and the diagnostics reports sent voluntarily. Its administrator must publish their own privacy policy; the serveur/README.md file says where and for how long each piece of data is kept, and how to erase it.

The base itself

The base records its temperature history and its log in its own memory. If it is configured to send alerts or a heartbeat to a web address (webhook, ntfy, healthchecks…), these messages go to the service you specified and contain the base's identifier, the temperatures and the alert text. During an update "from GitHub", the base downloads the binary from GitHub's servers, which see its IP address.

Your rights

As the publisher holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on their side: everything is on your device and at the services you chose. For any question: to be completed (contact address).
