Privacy

← App

This page says which data the Terrariums app and the Exotic World server process, what for, where, and for how long. The source code of the app, the base and the server is published on GitHub: you can check what follows there. The French version (Confidentialité) is the authoritative text.

Controller

The app's publisher, named in the legal notice, is responsible for the data the Exotic World server processes. For any question or request: to be completed (contact address).

On the Exotic World server, with your account

When you sign in with your e-mail address, the app goes through the publisher's server (exotic-world.duckdns.org), which your bases join too. It records:

  • your account: e-mail address, public keys of your passkeys (with their name and dates of use), hash of a password if there is one, preferences (monthly logbook by e-mail, names given to your bases);
  • your sessions, as hashes, and your notification subscriptions: the technical address provided by your browser, with what it says about itself (name, version, system);
  • your bases: those attached to your account and those you share, with the invited accounts and their rights;
  • your bases' history: temperatures and setpoints of each zone, one point per minute, and what each base went through (connections, faults, restarts, updates);
  • support: the diagnostics reports you send, your requests to support, their answers and the team's notes; during an access to the base that you open to support, the temporary key of that access and what is done with it;
  • an audit log of the actions on accounts and bases, where addresses are replaced by a pseudonymous reference.

Your bases' pairing code stays on your phone: commands are signed with it. It is sent to the server only once, when you attach a base to your account, so that the server can check with the base that you know it; the server does not keep it. The sign-in code received by e-mail is only kept in memory, as a hash. To slow down attacks, the server counts in memory, over a quarter of an hour, the attempts of each IP address; it does not record IP addresses.

What for

  • Providing the service you asked for: sign-in, control and history of your bases, notifications, sharing, support, and the monthly logbook if you turn it on. Legal basis: performance of the service you requested (article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Protecting the service: audit log, attempt limits, backups. Legal basis: the publisher's legitimate interest in securing the service and being able to account for what was done (article 6(1)(f)).

No data is sold, or used for advertising or profiling.

For how long

  • Account, attached bases, preferences, subscriptions, support requests: until the account is deleted.
  • Sessions: 30 days without use. Sign-in code: 10 minutes.
  • Bases' history and events: 400 days.
  • Diagnostics reports and audit log: 12 months.
  • Access to a base opened to support: 24 hours by default, 7 days at most; its key is erased at the end.
  • Backups: one per night, kept 14 days on the server and 30 days in Google Cloud's storage. Erased data therefore disappears from them 30 days later at the latest.

Deleting your account immediately erases the account, its sessions, its passkeys, its subscriptions, its support requests and its reports, as well as the history of the bases that have no other owner left.

Where, and with whom

  • Google Cloud (contract with Google Cloud France SARL) hosts the server and its backups, in a Google data centre in South Carolina, United States.
  • Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, Paris) sends the e-mails: sign-in codes, and the monthly logbook if you turn it on. It receives your address and the content of the message.
  • Your browser's notification service (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft) carries the notifications. They are encrypted: it does not read their content.
  • Netlify (United States) hosts the site. It keeps the usual technical logs of a web server: IP address, requested page, date.

Your data therefore leaves the European Union. These transfers rely on the safeguards these providers offer: the European Commission's standard contractual clauses and, for certified companies, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

Without an account, with your own broker

The app can also talk directly to an MQTT broker of your choice (advanced setting). The Exotic World server is then not involved: the messages between your bases and the app go through that broker, under its own terms. They contain the temperatures, the setpoints, the commands and each base's identifier.

On your device

The app keeps in the browser's local storage the address of the server or broker, your session (or the broker's username and password), your bases with their pairing code, your preferences, and the temperatures received to draw the charts. Uninstalling the app or clearing the site data deletes them. With a lock code, this configuration is encrypted with that code (AES-256), which is stored nowhere. The site sets no cookie and embeds no tracker, advertising, audience measurement or third-party script. The diagnostics report is only produced if you ask for it; it contains no password and no pairing code.

The base itself

The base records its temperature history and its log in its own memory. If it is configured to send alerts or a heartbeat to a web address (webhook, ntfy, healthchecks…), these messages go to the service you specified and contain the base's identifier, the temperatures and the alert text. During an update "from GitHub", the base downloads the binary from GitHub's servers, which see its IP address. For automatic updates it also reads there, once a day, a small file describing the latest published version: the request carries nothing beyond what a browser sends (IP address, "terrarium/<version>" as the software), neither an identifier nor a temperature. Turning automatic updates off does not stop this check, which is also used to warn you; it stops with a firmware built without a manifest.

Your rights

You can access your data, have it corrected or erased, restrict its use, object to processing based on legitimate interest, and get it back in a readable format. In the app, Settings → Your account → My data lets you export your whole account (JSON file) and delete it. For everything else, including the support notes and the audit log lines about you: to be completed (contact address). You can also lodge a complaint with the CNIL, the French data protection authority (cnil.fr).
